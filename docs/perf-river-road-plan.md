# River beds and road grades - plan

Target: `arnis-triagefix`, branch `perf/speed-to-worldgen-phase5`, HEAD `1597ce8c` (all file:line anchors verified at that commit, including the critic-pass corrections folded in below). Both features are deliberate output changes, flag-gated and default-off; both maps' root-cause findings were spot-verified against the code before this plan was finalized.

Scope guard, restated up front because it is non-negotiable: the bed work applies to RIVERS ONLY (`waterway=river/stream/canal`-family ways and `water=river`-family / riverbank polygons). Lakes and oceans keep today's bed generation byte-for-byte, including their sqrt terraces, shoal ring, bank wobble and dunes. Where a river meets a lake or the sea, the river profile blends out to the legacy depth over a band; it never steps. Because OSM river centerlines are routinely drawn through lakes and past the coastline, "lakes untouched" is enforced by explicit mask clipping and suppression (below), not assumed from tags.

## The two defects

### Defect 1 - terraced river beds ("dunes"/shelves under water)

The bed pipeline is tag-blind (it sees only ESA `LC_WATER`; river vs lake vs ocean is known nowhere at carve time) and its depth shape is intrinsically stepped. The visible terraces are the sum of five verified mechanisms:

1. **Integer sqrt tiers**: per-column depth = `local_max * sqrt((dt - shoal)/span)`, rounded to integer (`depth_from_dt`, `src/water_depth.rs:374-396`; this rounding is the 3.1.7 "bed-profile rounding"), with `local_max` width-tiered 2/3/4/6 (`polygon_local_max`, `water_depth.rs:354`) and capped at `MAX_WATER_DEPTH = 6` (`water_depth.rs:59`). Every integer crossing of the sqrt curve is one concentric shelf tracking the shoreline.
2. **Flat shoal ring**: DT < `SHOAL_DT_UNITS = 9` chamfer units (~3 blocks) forces depth 0 (`water_depth.rs:50`, test at `:375`). This flat ring is the anti-grade at the shore - the opposite of a soft bank.
3. **Bank wobble**: a deterministic +/-2-unit value-noise perturbation of the DT input (`ocean_depth_for_cell`, `water_depth.rs:399-414`, noise at `:412`) wiggles the shelf contours by ~0.7 blocks.
4. **Dune pass**: `place_underwater_dunes` (`water_depth.rs:782-835`) adds 1-4 block bumps to any column with `depth >= 1 && !near_bridge` (gate `water_depth.rs:639-641`, mirrored in `dune_bump_at` at `:653`).
5. **Water-surface contour steps**: the carve anchors at per-column `water_y = ground.water_level` (`water_depth.rs:895`). A "flowing" component keeps the DEM's local-median gradient quantized to integer Y (`src/elevation/postprocess.rs:362-397`), so terrain contour steps translate the entire carve stack vertically mid-river.

Explicitly NOT a cause: `ground_generation.rs` land-surface noise never touches water columns (shrubland/bare/snow passes all skip water). The "noise texture in the bed" is items 1-4; item 5 is a surface artifact the bed inherits.

### Defect 2 - 1-block steps across the road surface mid-segment

1. **Quantized terrain input**: `Ground::level` (`src/ground.rs:614`) computes a smooth bilinear value inside `interpolate_height` (`ground.rs:694-719`) and rounds it to integer internally (`:717`). A road on a gentle grade crosses the 0.5 rounding contour mid-segment. A second property of the same accessor matters for the fix: `get_data_coordinates` clamps coordinates to the render bbox (`ground.rs:685-690`, ratios clamped 0..1; `cover_class` clamps identically, `:509-522`), so any terrain read outside the bbox returns edge-clamped values.
2. **No longitudinal grading exists**: the road's Y machinery is purely cross-sectional (`perpendicular_median_ground_y`, `src/element_processing/highways.rs:122-166`; width median from `perpendicular_median_raw` `:29-54`; per-station fill via `precompute_row_medians` `:69`). The only along-length smoothing is a 3-tap median explicitly documented to pass monotone steps through (`highways.rs:107-111`). Nothing anywhere limits dY per block of run, so the width-median carries the full-width cliff straight through.
3. **Embankment cementing**: the ground pass builds terrain at exactly the stepped override Y (`src/ground_generation.rs:844`, underfill `:917-959`), turning the step into permanent landscape.

Secondary step sources, all real and all addressed structurally by the design: diagonal-travel stamp overlap (perpendicular strips centered on the centerline cross-axis, `highways.rs:82-88`, give one column two Ys); per-segment `dir_horizontal` axis flips at bends past 45 degrees; junction overrides resolved last-writer-wins (`src/world_editor/mod.rs:809`); median sampling reads `get_ground_level`, which folds in prior roads' overrides (`mod.rs:748-756`) - an order-coupled feedback loop; and at reduced scale `block_range` floors to 0 (`highways.rs:1186-1188`), so `flatten_width` (`highways.rs:1315`) is false and narrow roads get no flattening at all.

## U-shaped bed design (final)

**Governing decisions.** Compose, don't replace: `compute_big_water_field` / `depth_from_dt` / `carve_lc_water_pass` stay byte-identical for every non-river column; a new per-render `RiverBedField` overrides depth only inside a river mask, so the lakes/oceans-untouched rule holds by construction for polygon-mapped water, and by explicit clip/suppression for the centerline-through-lake cases. Depth-only, never carve-creation: the field modifies depth only for columns that already carve today (the LC_WATER pass, `water_depth.rs:838-943`, and the OSM relation scanline fill, `src/element_processing/water_areas.rs:397-483`). It never introduces carving where none exists, which keeps the historically failed line-waterway carving dead (`waterways.rs:1-8`: grooves, cut trees, floaters; note the comment at `data_processing.rs:466-468` referencing `carve_waterway_region` is STALE - no such carve exists, do not cite it). Streams absent from ESA LC_WATER stay flat surface ribbons, as today. `water_distance` (`land_cover.rs:1243-1296`) is NOT reused: grid-resolution, capped at 15 cells, cell-local.

**River classification** (built once per render, pre-tiling, next to the `compute_big_water_field` call at `src/data_processing.rs:464`), by a tag scan over ALL parsed elements - NOT by "what reaches water_areas.rs": the dispatch routes any way with a `natural` key to `natural.rs` (surface paint only, `data_processing.rs:104-112`); only relations (`:235-245`) and `waterway=dock` ways (`:126-135`) reach water_areas. Classes:

- Line rivers: `ProcessedWay` with `waterway` in {river, stream, canal, brook, fairway, flowline} passing `is_drawable_waterway` semantics (`waterways.rs:127-132`); `ditch`/`drain` excluded (below useful resolution, today's behavior fine). Footprint reuses `compute_waterway_field`'s geometry only (`waterways.rs:214-255`): bresenham centerline x `scaled_half_width(waterway_width(way), scale)` (`waterways.rs:74-102`, width clamp 128 at `:42`), with the `ground_level(x,z) <= seg_water_y + BANK_TOLERANCE` gate (`waterways.rs:246`) explicitly DROPPED - it is Ground-dependent and bbox-clamped; the apply-time would-carve intersect bounds the mask instead. `hw` is floored at 1.0 (`scaled_half_width` returns 0 below scale 0.3 and <=1 below 0.7, `waterways.rs:111-118`), so `t = d_blocks/hw` never divides by zero.
- Polygon rivers: (a) relations whose `water` tag VALUE is in {river, canal, stream} (value already in `rel.tags`, currently only key-checked; oxbow is a lake, excluded); (b) closed ways with `natural=water` + `water` in {river, canal, stream} - the most common river-polygon form, which never reaches water_areas.rs; they get a mask-only scanline rasterization and their carve remains the LC pass; (c) `waterway=riverbank` ways, today dropped by `is_channel_waterway` (`waterways.rs:79`): mask-only scanline as well (none of these draw anything themselves).
- Everything else (bare `natural=water`, ESA-only water, coastline ocean) = not-river = legacy path untouched.

**Line-mask post-processing**, in order, before any DT is computed (this is the findings-3/8 machinery):

- Clip: subtract from the line stamp the interiors of element-derived NON-river water polygons (bare `natural=water`/`bay` ways and relations without a river-family `water` value) and the coastline ocean side. Rings and node lists are unclipped, so the clip is tile-safe. This removes the centerline-drawn-through-a-mapped-lake and past-the-coast stamps that would otherwise override lake/sea bed columns.
- Embedding suppression: for columns whose mask membership comes ONLY from a line stamp (no river polygon), compute `land_d` = chamfer DT to the nearest non-water LC cell on the same halo lattice; if `land_d > hw + 4`, remove the column from R. This kills the striped-hybrid case (wide LC-water river carrying only a default-width-10 centerline would otherwise get a narrow override ribbon in permanent half-blend, plausibly worse than today - those rivers now keep the legacy bed wholesale, never worse than today) and it also removes centerline stamps inside ESA-only lakes, which have no polygon to subtract. Residual: inside an ESA-only lake a stamp ribbon can survive only within `hw + 4` of the LC shore, where both profiles are shallow; stated in release notes. Widening `hw` from an LC-derived signal (making wide untagged rivers fully smooth) is deferred to `v2`. Because `land_d` reads LC, and LC reads beyond the render bbox clamp (`cover_class`, `ground.rs:509-522`), the suppression decision within `hw + 4` of a cell border is tile-invariant only if the LC grid covers the halo - R6's scope now includes this pad; without it, a documented bounded residual (the flip only chooses between two valid beds).
- Removal happens before the bed DT so mask, d, hw and blend all see one consistent footprint.

**The `RiverBedField`**, one lattice covering the cell bbox padded by halo `H = min(96, ceil(64*scale) + 32)` blocks (64 = max scaled waterway half-width, 32 = max confluence band). Rasterization uses full unclipped node lists and ring geometry, which is exactly why the halo works.

1. Mask `R(x,z)`: 1 bit, from classification + clip + suppression, intersected at apply time with "would carve today". Would-carve is respecified per carve path: LC_WATER-pass columns, or inside a relation's scanline fill span; way-polygons and riverbanks have no fill span of their own - their intersect is the LC pass only.
2. Distance `d(x,z)`: chamfer-3-4 DT (`chamfer_3_4_dt`, `water_depth.rs:304`, reused verbatim) seeded 0 at every non-mask lattice cell; `d_blocks = d/3.0`. The u8 chamfer saturates at 255 units ~ 85 blocks; that is the effective ceiling on `d_blocks` and hence on the polygon `hw` below - harmless, since depth saturates at `hw >= 30`, but stated.
3. Local half-width `hw(x,z)`: line rivers stamp `hw = max(scaled_half_width(waterway_width(way), scale), 1)` per column, overlapping ways take max (order-independent). Polygon rivers: `hw = max(d_blocks over a square window of radius W = 16*scale)`, floored at own `d_blocks`, ceilinged by the u8 DT saturation above. Where both apply: max. This is the tag-derived, element-keyed, unclipped width signal the map says does not exist yet (the established Overture-hints tile-safe pattern).
4. **Depth formula** (f64 until the final round):
   - `t = clamp(d_blocks / hw, 0, 1)` with `hw >= 1` guaranteed.
   - bank-roundness exponent `q = 1.0 + 0.5 * min(hw/30, 1) * min(scale, 1)`; narrow stream q ~ 1.0 (tight curve), wide 1:1 river q = 1.5 (long soft banks). This is "roundness scales with width and map scale".
   - shape `s = 3*tau^2 - 2*tau^3` with `tau = t^q`: smoothstep, zero slope at the shore (soft entry, no shoal cliff) and zero slope at center (broad rounded bottom). A literal parabola is steepest at the shore, the opposite of the ask; smoothstep is the "rounded parabola" that matches the reference cross-sections.
   - depth cap `D = clamp(6 * (hw/30)^0.7, 1.0, D_max)`, `D_max = MAX_WATER_DEPTH = 6` at scale >= 0.5, `SMALL_SCALE_MAX_DEPTH = 5` below (`water_depth.rs:99`). Table: hw=3 -> D~1.2; hw=10 -> ~2.8; hw=20 -> ~4.5; hw>=30 -> 6.
   - `depth_f = D*s`, then one 3x3 tent smooth of the lattice (rounds the chamfer facets - the "smoothed shoreline" anchor), then `depth = round(depth_f).clamp(0, D_max)`.
   - NO shoal ring (`SHOAL_DT_UNITS` not consulted for river columns), NO bank wobble and NO dunes OUTSIDE confluence bands (river columns never call `ocean_depth_for_cell`, bypassing `:412` structurally; dune gate change below). Inside a confluence band the blend target re-imports attenuated legacy noise - see point 5; that is the stated price of exact continuity, not an oversight.
   - Peak analytic bank slope is `1.5*D/hw` per block: ~0.3 for hw>=30 and D<=1.3 for hw<=4, so adjacent-column `|delta depth| <= 1` everywhere by construction (still asserted in tests).
5. **Confluence blend** (river meets untouched lake/sea): second chamfer DT `m(x,z)` seeded 0 at every would-carve, non-mask water lattice cell adjacent to R, where water-ness is decided by element-derived non-river polygons, the coastline ocean side, the clipped/suppressed stamp columns from the post-processing step, and LC_WATER cells. The LC term is what lets a river blend into an ESA-only lake; it carries the same bbox-clamp caveat as suppression (tile-invariant with R6's LC halo pad, else a documented residual whenever a confluence lies within `B + tent` of a seam). Band `B = clamp(2*hw, 8, 32)` blocks. Final river-column depth `= round(lerp(legacy_f, river_f, smoothstep(clamp(m/B, 0, 1))))` where `legacy_f = f64(bwf.depth_at(x,z))`. At the mask boundary m=0 the value equals the legacy depth exactly, so there is zero step into the lake bed, and the lake side is never written differently at all. Two consequences stated plainly: (a) within the band, `legacy_f` carries the bank wobble and sqrt terraces in attenuated form - river columns with `m < B` are not fully terrace-free; (b) `legacy_f` also carries BWF's cell-bbox DT + per-cell-fragment `comp_max` seam hazard (`water_depth.rs:250-255`), so river columns inside a band that straddles a cell border are not tile-invariant - a bounded residual (only `m < B`, only near borders), measured by the new confluence-at-seam A/B test. Outside bands, `comp_max` contamination is irrelevant for river columns and stays exactly as-is for lake columns.

**Plug points.** Build: `data_processing.rs:~464`, `compute_river_bed_field(&parsed_elements, &ground, &xzbbox, scale, args.river_bed)`. Apply in both carve callers: `carve_lc_water_region` (`water_depth.rs:912-941`) and `scanline_fill_water` (`water_areas.rs:452-481`) substitute `river_field.depth_override(x,z)` for `bwf.depth_at(x,z)` and pass a new `is_river: bool` through `carve_water_column_with_flags` (`water_depth.rs:490`). Noise exemptions for `is_river`: dune gate becomes `depth >= 1 && !near_bridge && !is_river` (`water_depth.rs:639-641`), mirrored in `dune_bump_at` (`:653`) so vegetation sits on the smooth bed (vegetation gate `:644-646` kept - it adds no height noise). Bed palette noise (`water_depth.rs:534-620`) is block-type-only, zero height effect: kept, but river columns force depth<=1 -> SAND and skip the MAGMA/SOUL_SAND arms (sea-floor reads, wrong in a river). Land-side shore band (`ground_generation.rs:682-810`) untouched (palette-only, fine). **Clearance interaction (corrected)**: `estimate_max_carve_depth` (`water_depth.rs:448-485`, called from `ground.rs:263`) returns the legacy width-tiered bound derived from the map's max LC DT - NOT `MAX_WATER_DEPTH` - so in Measured mode a tag-derived `hw` can legitimately exceed the reservation (width-40 way crossing a 12-block LC strip reaches t~1, depth up to 6 vs a reserved 2-3) and the `.min(water_y - MIN_Y - 2)` clamp would silently flatten the bed. Fix, part of R5: with `--river-bed v1` on, Measured returns `max(legacy estimate, scale depth cap D_max)`; flag off is byte-identical; Fixed/`max` mode (the Bucharest render) already reserves `MAX_WATER_DEPTH` and needs nothing. The existing debug_assert stays as enforcement and a Measured-mode test is added. At scale < 0.5 river columns use this formula INSTEAD of `bowl_depth_small_scale` (`water_depth.rs:433`); non-river columns keep the bowl.

**Inherited limits, documented not fixed**: the bed anchors at per-column `water_y`, so where a flowing river's surface steps (per-cell `level_water_surfaces` gradient - the unexplained Navodari family), the whole smooth profile translates 1 block with it. Additionally `water_level`'s steep-terrain snap samples +/-3 neighbours (`ground.rs:628-664`) which clamp at the bbox edge, so `water_y` itself can differ within 3 blocks of a seam on steep banks - pre-existing, inherited equally by the new bed. The profile guarantees smoothness relative to the surface; the surface itself is out of scope.

## Road grade design (final)

**Per-way longitudinal profile**, computed before the node loop (insert between the way-length block at `highways.rs:1227-1241`, where the exact `total_bresenham_length` already exists, and the node loop at `:1290`):

1. **Station list**: concatenate `bresenham_line` output for all segments into `stations: Vec<(i32,i32)>` indexed by `tds`, reproducing the placement loop's exact `tds = cumulative_distance_from_start + point_index` mapping (`highways.rs:1471-1474`; accumulation `cumulative_distance_from_start += segment_length - 1` at `:1950`). Because of that `- 1`, non-bridge shared segment endpoints repeat at the SAME tds (`skip_first = 0`, `:1466-1470`): the builder must OVERWRITE at equal tds, never append, or indices desync along the rest of the way; the G3 unit test asserts `station_count == total_bresenham_length`.
2. **Base samples** (float): `base[i]` = median of `terrain_level_f64` over the `2*block_range+1` perpendicular strip at each station (same strip geometry as `perpendicular_median_raw`, `:29-54`) - sampling RAW terrain, never `get_ground_level`, which severs the override-feedback loop at `mod.rs:748-756`. Requires a new pre-round accessor `Ground::level_f64` exposed as `WorldEditor::terrain_level_f64` beside `terrain_level` (`mod.rs:777`); the refactor lands INSIDE `interpolate_height` (`ground.rs:694-719`), which currently rounds internally at `:717` - a real refactor, not a wrapper around `level` (`:614`). Building from the float field means the profile sees the smooth surface the rounding destroyed. Caveat that drives the windowing below: samples at stations outside the render bbox are edge-clamped (`get_data_coordinates`, `ground.rs:685-690`), i.e. wrong, and wrong differently in each neighbouring cell.
3. **Junction pins**: for every way node that is a junction, `pin = median of terrain_level_f64 over a fixed 3x3 window at (node.x, node.z)`. Deliberately width- and way-independent: a pure function of node world coordinates + DEM, so every way at that junction, in every tile and cell, computes the identical pin (subject to the same bbox-clamp caveat, handled by the windowing). `build_highway_connectivity_map` (`highways.rs:365-400`) currently records way ENDPOINTS only; extend it (or a sibling map) to all nodes so mid-way T-junctions pin too.
4. **Slope-limited profile, closed-form and window-local** (replaces the earlier forward/backward relaxation, which was neither direction-symmetric nor pin-preserving - a forward-first clamp biases toward the way's start and destroys mid-way pins). Max grade `g = 1/N_eff` blocks per block of run; `N` by class: motorway/trunk/primary 12, secondary/tertiary 8, residential/unclassified/service 6, footway/path/track 4; `highway=steps` excluded entirely (stairs are supposed to step). `N_eff = max(2, round(N * scale))`. Construction:
   - **Anchors (DEM-influence bound, the tile-invariance fix)**: hard constraints `p[j] = base[j]` at every station with absolute `tds ≡ 0 (mod K)`, `K = 64`. tds is way-intrinsic (counted from the way's first node, whole-element-assigned to every tile/cell), so anchor positions are identical everywhere. All computation below runs per anchor-delimited window, so no base or pin value influences the profile more than K stations away. Without this, the slope clamp propagates edge-clamped DEM garbage unboundedly into the cell - the killer defect of the previous draft.
   - **Constraint set** `C` = junction pins + anchors. Feasibility: walk constraints in tds order; for any adjacent pair with `|delta c| / delta d > g`, use `g_int = |delta c| / delta d` within that interval (never worse than today). Interval-based, hence direction-free.
   - **Envelopes**: `L[i] = max_j(c_j - g*d_ij)`, `U[i] = min_j(c_j + g*d_ij)` over constraints j in the window (two sweeps each).
   - **Symmetric Lipschitz projection of base**: `A[i] = min_j(base[j] + g*d_ij)` (largest g-Lipschitz minorant of base), `B[i] = max_j(base[j] - g*d_ij)` (smallest g-Lipschitz majorant), `p = clamp((A+B)/2, L, U)`. Min/max/clamp of g-Lipschitz functions are g-Lipschitz, so p is grade-limited everywhere; at a constraint `L = U = c` so pins and anchors are held EXACTLY; `d_ij` is symmetric, so a reversed OSM way yields the mirror-identical profile by construction (still unit-tested, since way direction is arbitrary). Each of the four fields is a two-sweep O(n) infimal convolution per window.
   - Visual cost of anchors: on a sustained steep climb the profile rejoins the (float, step-free) terrain base every K blocks, so extreme grades are smoothed locally rather than globally - acceptable, since the defect is 1-block contour steps, not long grades.
5. Result `profile: Vec<f64>`, rounded to i32 only at placement. Rounding a slope-limited profile yields 1-block steps spaced >= `N_eff` blocks apart - the voxel-minimum ramp - instead of terrain-contour clusters.

**Junction reconciliation, order-free**: (a) the pin makes every way hit the same Y at the shared node column regardless of processing order, tile, or element set; (b) the override fold changes from last-writer-wins insert (`register_road_surface_y`, `mod.rs:809`) to min-Y-wins `entry.and_modify(|v| *v = (*v).min(y)).or_insert(y)`, and the tile merges (`merge_road_surface_overrides` / `_in_regions`, `mod.rs:818-838`) change from `extend` to the same min-fold - flag-gated. Min is commutative and associative, so the final override map is independent of element order, tile batch order, the LPT/band sort, `ARNIS_NO_BAND`, stream-to-disk eviction, and sequential-vs-tiled paths. Residual: within a junction fan two ways can still round to Ys differing by 1 (they agree exactly only at the node; at distance d they differ by <= 2*g*d before rounding). The ground pass follows the min-fold, so terrain is consistent; the only artifact is possible asphalt one block above folded ground from the higher way. Optional phase-2 pass: after highways complete, iterate override columns in SORTED key order and clear road-surface blocks above the final override Y (hook where overrides reach the main editor, `data_processing.rs:863, 897-902`). Ship phase 1 without it; measure visibility first.

**Consumption**: flag on + non-bridge way: `cell_y = profile[tds].round() as i32 + offset` replaces the `row_medians` base at `highways.rs:1585-1595`; skip `precompute_row_medians`. Because Y is now a pure function of `tds`, the diagonal-stamp-overlap disagreement and the per-segment axis-flip jump disappear structurally. Coverage extension: allow `block_range == 0` in the `flatten_width` gate (`highways.rs:1315`) when grading is on, so small-scale narrow roads (today: no flattening, no overrides) get graded Y and register their 1-wide column. Kept exclusions: bridges/ramps still take deck Y from `y_at` (`:1477-1480`) and never register; `register_ground_override = flatten && offset == 0` stays (`:1498`, applied `:1593`) so elevated sections never register (embankment explosion, `:1316-1323`); tunnels stay excluded; bridge-foot steps remain an acknowledged separate gap. `calculate_point_elevation` (`:2031`) and its approximate way length are untouched (offset ramps only); the profile uses the exact `total_bresenham_length`.

## Tile-invariance argument for each

Every past seam bug came from cell-local state; here is why two neighbouring master-origin cells produce the identical block, per feature - with every residual named rather than claimed away.

**Rivers.** The override depth for any column outside a confluence band is a pure function of four inputs, each provably identical in both cells:
1. Unclipped element geometry: mask, DT seeds, and line-river `hw` are rasterized from full node lists / rings / tag values into the halo lattice, NOT from the cell-cropped LC grid. Meld's seam-expanded fetch and arnis's whole-element tile assignment (`src/tile.rs:51, 116-127`; only writes are clipped) give both cells the same element data. The line-mask clip subtracts unclipped rings, so it is tile-safe too.
2. Master-anchored coordinates and `--seed`: the lattice is addressed in absolute block coords; the only noise in the whole path (legacy wobble/dunes) is removed for river columns outside bands, so the core river field is noise-free and RNG-free.
3. The chamfer DT over that lattice: for any column inside the cell proper, the nearest non-mask cell is within halo `H`, so `d` equals the true geometric distance in both cells; columns beyond depth saturation (t = 1) are insensitive to lattice-edge clipping. The windowed-max polygon `hw` is a windowed max of a tile-invariant field with `H >= W + tent reach + B_max`, hence tile-invariant. Overlapping-way max is order-independent.
4. Apply-time carve membership: the LC grid is master-origin anchored (`GridMapping::new_master`, `land_cover.rs:524-556`), same class per block in every cell.
This removes the map's BWF seam hazard (cell-bbox DT + per-cell-fragment `comp_max`) for river columns OUTSIDE confluence bands; inside a band (`m < B`) the blend target `legacy_f` re-imports it - a bounded residual, tested. Lake columns keep the existing documented hazard, unchanged, which is exactly the scope rule. Remaining caveats, stated not hidden: (a) `water_y` comes from per-cell `level_water_surfaces` (Navodari family), and `water_level`'s +/-3 steep-snap is bbox-clamped within 3 blocks of a seam - both pre-existing, inherited; (b) the OSM fetch halo (task R6): a way passing within `64*scale` blocks of a cell without entering its fetch bbox could exist in one cell's data only - audited and padded, or documented as a bounded residual; (c) LC reads beyond the render bbox clamp (`cover_class`, `ground.rs:509-522`), so the embedding-suppression decision and the LC-seeded part of `m` are tile-invariant only with R6's LC-grid halo pad - without it, a bounded near-seam residual confined to `hw + 4` (suppression) and `B + tent` (blend) of the border.

**Roads.** The profile Y at any station is a pure function of:
1. The full unclipped way node list (whole-element assignment as above) -> identical `stations` and `tds` in every tile and cell, including the same-tds overwrite at shared endpoints.
2. Master-origin DEM via `terrain_level_f64` (`ground_origin_x/z` set to the MAIN world origin in tile editors, `data_processing.rs:707`) - never `get_ground_level`, so the existing override-feedback coupling (road Y depending on which roads ran before, `mod.rs:748-756`) is severed, not inherited. Master anchoring alone is NOT sufficient: DEM reads outside the render bbox are edge-clamped (`ground.rs:685-690`), so cross-CELL invariance additionally requires the influence bound. The absolute-tds anchors cap any sample's influence at K stations; with a master-anchored DEM halo pad of `K + 8` blocks (K for window reach, 8 for the sampling strip / 3x3 pins - task G2), every sample that can influence an in-cell column reads true DEM in both cells, and profiles are byte-identical. Without the pad, the mismatch is bounded by local relief over K blocks and decays to zero at each anchor - documented residual, measured by test 5.
3. Junction pins = f(node coords, DEM) only - no dependence on widths, element-set contents, or order; any way sharing a node inside a cell's writable+halo area necessarily intersects its AABB and is whole-element-assigned, so both cells see the same junction set. Pin influence is also window-bounded.
4. Min-fold override merge: commutative, so cross-tile merge order, `ARNIS_NO_BAND`, RAM-dependent eviction, and sequential-vs-tiled paths all converge to one map (this outright deletes the map's hazard 2, machine-dependent batch ordering, for road columns).
Determinism: no RNG, no wall-clock anywhere in the profile; the projection is closed-form (no iteration-count sensitivity); the override map is point-looked-up only; the optional phase-2 pass iterates sorted keys. Intra-run, tiles share one run-wide Ground, so the clamp issue does not arise between tiles - only between Meld cells. Caveat stated in the PR: in Meld LOCAL-elevation mode (as in the defect render) the per-cell datum shifts absolute Ys per cell - that is the pre-existing elevation-lock seam class, out of scope; master-origin elevation mode plus the G2 DEM pad is the invariance-guaranteed configuration.

## Flags, golden hashes and rollout

These are deliberate output changes and cannot be hash-neutral. Management:

- **Two independent flags, default `off` = byte-identical.** `--river-bed off|v1` and `--road-grade off|on`, both plumbed like `water_carve_clearance` (`args.rs:400-411`) / `--road-detail` (`args.rs:459-472`), each with the `gui.rs` Args field and `validate_args` handling (cave-zone-map gotcha). `off` returns an empty river field / legacy median path AND legacy last-writer override semantics (the min-fold is gated too) AND the legacy Measured clearance estimate, so `off` is strictly hash-neutral and every existing golden stands.
- **Versioned, never silently retuned.** `v1` in the flag value is the version story: any future retune of q/D/B/N/K tables - including the deferred LC-derived hw widening for untagged wide rivers - is `v2`/`v3`, never a changed output under the same value. The flag values are recorded in the world build-hash metadata (3.1.7 build-hash precedent) so a world self-describes its grading/bed version.
- **Golden gates.** The existing golden_hash run stays flag-off and must pass byte-identical (remember: golden_hash.sh never rebuilds - full rebuild before any bake). Two new golden variants are baked after visual sign-off: a river fixture with `--river-bed v1`, and a road fixture with `--road-grade on`.
- **Meld rollout.** Meld flips its render invocation to `--river-bed v1 --road-grade on` in the release that ships this, with release notes stating: hash break for river-bearing cells and for all road-bearing cells (embankment terrain moves); polygon-mapped lake/ocean-only cells remain hash-identical even with `--river-bed v1` on, which is itself a shipped regression test; ESA-only rivers with no OSM river tagging keep the legacy terraced bed, and so do wide LC rivers carrying only a default-width centerline (embedding suppression) - correct by scope, visually mixed by region; a centerline crossing an ESA-only lake can retain a shore-hugging override ribbon within `hw + 4` of the LC shore (bounded residual). In Measured clearance mode the reserved floor deepens to the river cap when `--river-bed v1` is on (flag off unchanged). Old worlds heal only on cell regenerate, as always.

## Risks

1. **OSM fetch / LC grid halo gap (R6)**: a river way passing within `64*scale` blocks of a cell without entering its fetch bbox exists in one cell's data only; separately, LC reads beyond the render bbox clamp (`cover_class`), so embedding suppression (within `hw + 4` of a border) and LC-seeded blend `m` (within `B + tent`) can differ across cells. All rare, bounded, fixable with fetch/grid padding; if unpadded, documented residuals with bounds stated - a suppression flip only chooses between two valid beds.
2. **tds mapping fidelity (G3)**: any off-by-one between the station list and the placement loop desyncs profile from placement along the whole way - specifically the shared-endpoint same-tds repeat (`:1950`), which appends would double. Mitigated by overwrite-at-equal-tds, the count assert, the dedicated byte-exact unit test and a debug assert in the cell loop; G4/G6 held until it passes.
3. **Confluence bands are not terrace-free and not seam-proof**: within `m < B` the blend target carries attenuated legacy wobble/terraces and BWF's cell-bbox/`comp_max` hazard. Deliberate (exact continuity at m=0 requires matching legacy), bounded to the band, measured by the new at-seam A/B.
4. **Centerline-through-lake cases**: mapped lakes/coastline are protected by the clip; ESA-only lakes rely on embedding suppression, leaving a possible override ribbon within `hw + 4` of the LC shore. Bounded, fixture-covered, release-noted.
5. **ESA-only and centerline-only wide rivers**: cells whose rivers carry no OSM river tagging, and wide LC rivers whose only tagging is a narrow centerline (suppressed), keep the legacy terraced bed - correct by scope, never worse than today, visually mixed by region; LC-derived hw widening is the named `v2` candidate.
6. **Road DEM clamp near seams**: without the G2 pad, profiles within K of a Meld cell border can mismatch by up to the local relief over K blocks, decaying to zero at anchors; with the pad, byte-identical. The anchor windowing itself trades global grade smoothing for locality (base rejoin every K=64 blocks on extreme grades).
7. **Junction fan lip**: <= 1-block asphalt lip above min-folded ground where two graded ways overlap off-node. Terrain is consistent (min-fold); phase-2 pass (G8) exists if visible.
8. **Water-surface steps remain**: flowing-river surface gradients (per-cell `level_water_surfaces`, the Navodari family) still translate the smooth profile vertically by 1 block at surface steps, and `water_level`'s +/-3 steep-snap is bbox-clamped within 3 blocks of a seam. Out of scope; the bed is smooth relative to the surface.
9. **Very wide untagged polygon rivers (> ~2H across)**: DT lattice-edge clipping can differ per cell deep in the interior; depth is saturated at D there for nearly all columns (and the u8 DT cap ~85 blocks saturates hw anyway), residual is at most a 1-tier difference in a narrow annulus; documented bound, no known real-world instance at H = 96.
10. **Measured clearance under-reservation**: fixed by R5 (max(legacy, river cap) under the flag) and tested in step 6; the failure mode without it is a silently flattened bed at the reserved floor, not a crash.
11. **Bridge feet still step**: deliberate exclusion (registering decks would turn bridges into embankments); acknowledged unmanaged transition, separate future work.
12. **Meld LOCAL-elevation renders**: per-cell datum shifts absolute Ys across cells for both features - pre-existing elevation-lock seam class, unchanged by this work; master-origin elevation mode (plus the G2 pad) is the invariance-guaranteed configuration.
13. **Golden discipline**: golden_hash.sh never rebuilds, and a Meld release can publish partial assets when upload-artifact flakes - full rebuild before baking goldens, full rerun (never rerun-failed-jobs) on release.
14. **Harness plumbing (G10/R7)**: the A/B rigs exist but were built for other branches; budget includes adaptation, and the tile-count A/B doubles as the safety net if adaptation uncovers drift.
